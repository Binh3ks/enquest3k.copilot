## III. WEEK-BASED CEFR LEVEL GUIDELINES

**CRITICAL**: Use this table to select correct CEFR level and grammar based on week number:

| Week Range | CEFR Level | Grammar Focus | Vocabulary Tier |
|------------|------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 1-18 | A0/A0++ | Present Simple Be, Can/Can't | Tier 1 only |
| 19-40 | A1 | Past Simple (Was/Were, -ed, irregular) | Tier 1 + basic Tier 2 |
| 41-54 | A1+ | Future (going to), Present Perfect chunks | Tier 1-2 mix |
| 55-70 | A2 | Because/So, First Conditional, Modals | Tier 2 primary |

### LEVEL DETAILS

### A0/A0++ (Weeks 1-18)

**VOCABULARY**:
- Tier 1 only: concrete nouns, basic verbs
- Max 2 syllables preferred
- Examples: book, pen, school, happy, go, see, have

**GRAMMAR**:
- Present simple ONLY
- Subject + Be: "I am", "She is", "They are"
- Subject + Verb: "I have", "I like", "I go"
- Questions: "What is...?", "Where is...?", "Can I...?"
- NO: past tense, future tense, conditionals, passive voice

**SENTENCES**:
- **Easy Mode**: 5-8 words per sentence
  - Structure: Simple sentences, compound with "and", "but", "so"
  - Example: "I go to school and I play."
  
- **Advanced Mode**: 8-14 words per sentence (longer OK if A0 vocab)
  - Structure: Compound AND complex sentences allowed
  - Can use: "because", "and", "but", "so"
  - Example: "My backpack is heavy because I carry my book and notebook every day." (14 words ✅)
  - NO: "although", "however", relative clauses (save for Phase 2)

**QUESTION PATTERNS (A0 ONLY)**:
```
✅ ALLOWED:
- What is this?
- Where is the book?
- Is this a pen?
- Can I play?
- Do you like school?
- How are you?

❌ NOT ALLOWED:
- How do they go to school? (too complex)
- What does it do? (too complex)
- Where can I find...? (too complex)
- May I do both? (too formal)
```

---

### A1 (Weeks 19-40)

**VOCABULARY**:
- Tier 1 core + basic Tier 2 (common adjectives, time expressions)
- Examples: yesterday, last week, morning, afternoon, surprised, excited

**GRAMMAR**:
- **Weeks 19-25**: Past Simple with Was/Were
  - "I was at home." "They were happy."
- **Weeks 26-32**: Past Simple Regular verbs (-ed)
  - "I played football." "She watched TV."
- **Weeks 33-40**: Past Simple Irregular verbs
  - "I went to school." "She ate lunch." "They had fun."
- Still use Present Simple for routines
- Questions: "Where did you go?" "What did you do?"

**SENTENCES**:
- **Easy Mode**: 6-10 words
  - Example: "Yesterday I went to the park."
- **Advanced Mode**: 10-15 words
  - Example: "Last week I visited my grandma and we ate delicious food together."

---

### A1+ (Weeks 41-54)

**VOCABULARY**:
- Tier 1-2 balanced mix
- Time expressions: tomorrow, next week, in the future
- Examples: prepare, finish, start, improve

**GRAMMAR**:
- **Future with going to**: "I'm going to visit my friend."
- **Want to + verb**: "I want to learn English."
- **Present Perfect chunks** (formulaic only):
  - "I have been to the zoo." "Have you ever...?"
- Mix all three tenses (present, past, future)

**SENTENCES**:
- **Easy Mode**: 8-12 words
- **Advanced Mode**: 12-18 words
  - Example: "Tomorrow I'm going to help my mom because I want to be a good son."

---

### A2 (Weeks 55-70)

**VOCABULARY**:
- Tier 2 becomes primary
- Abstract concepts: opinion, reason, result, choice
- Examples: consequence, decision, argument, persuade

**GRAMMAR**:
- **Because/So**: "I study hard because I want good grades." "It rained, so I stayed home."
- **First Conditional**: "If I study, I will pass."
- **Modals**: should, could, might, must
- **OREO persuasion structure** (Week 67+):
  - Opinion: "I think homework is important."
  - Reason: "It helps us practice."
  - Example: "For example, math homework..."
  - Opinion: "That's why I believe..."

**SENTENCES**:
- **Easy Mode**: 10-14 words
- **Advanced Mode**: 14-20 words
  - Example: "If students do homework regularly, they will understand the lesson better and get higher scores."

---

### A2+ (Weeks 71-98)

**VOCABULARY**:
- Tier 2 + academic subject vocabulary
- Science: absorb, reflect, energy, ecosystem
- History: ancient, civilization, empire, trade
- Examples: photosynthesis, democracy, economy

**GRAMMAR**:
- **Passive Voice** (introduced Week 72):
  - "Sunlight is absorbed by plants."
  - "The pyramids were built thousands of years ago."
- **Zero Conditional**: "If you heat water, it boils."
- **Used to**: "People used to travel by horse."
- **Comparative/Superlative**: more/most, -er/-est

**SENTENCES**:
- **Easy Mode**: 12-16 words
- **Advanced Mode**: 16-25 words
  - Example: "When sunlight is absorbed by plant leaves, the energy is used to make food through photosynthesis."

---

### B1 (Weeks 99-112)

**VOCABULARY**:
- Tier 2-3 mix, academic vocabulary dominant
- Economics: supply, demand, consumer, profit
- Critical thinking: analyze, evaluate, compare, contrast
- Examples: sustainable, renewable, inequality, regulation

**GRAMMAR**:
- **Past Perfect**: "I had finished my homework before dinner."
- **Relative Clauses**: "Students who study hard get better grades."
- **Second Conditional**: "If I were rich, I would travel the world."
- **Gerunds/Infinitives**: "I enjoy reading." "I need to study."
- Research essay structures

**SENTENCES**:
- **Easy Mode**: 14-18 words
- **Advanced Mode**: 18-30 words
  - Example: "Before the invention of currency, people who wanted to trade goods had to use the barter system, which was inefficient."

---

### B1+ (Weeks 113-156) - DEBATE & SYNTHESIS PHASE

**VOCABULARY**:
- Academic Tier 3 dominant
- Debate: counter-argument, rebuttal, concession, nuance
- Critical analysis: synthesize, evaluate, fallacy, bias
- Examples: generalization, correlation, causation, credibility

**GRAMMAR**:
- All previous grammar + advanced structures:
  - **Nuanced thesis**: "While homework can be stressful, it ultimately benefits students because..."
  - **Concession + Counter**: "Although some argue X, the evidence shows Y."
  - **Citation integration**: "According to Smith (2020), students who..."
  - **Qualifiers**: Some, Many, Often (to avoid overgeneralization)

**DEBATE ESSAY STRUCTURE** (5 paragraphs):
1. **Introduction**: Hook + Background + Thesis with nuance
2. **Body 1**: Main argument with evidence + examples
3. **Body 2**: Second argument with research support
4. **Counter-argument**: Acknowledge opposing view + refute with evidence
5. **Conclusion**: Restate thesis + synthesis + call to action

**CRITICAL PHRASES**:
- Citation: "According to...", "Research shows...", "Statistics indicate..."
- Concession: "While it's true that...", "Although some believe..."
- Rebuttal: "However, the evidence suggests...", "On the contrary..."
- Qualification: "In many cases...", "Generally speaking...", "To some extent..."
- Polite disagreement: "With all due respect...", "I see your point, but..."

**SENTENCES**:
- **Easy Mode**: 16-22 words
- **Advanced Mode**: 20-40 words (complex, multi-clause)
  - Example: "While some educators argue that homework creates unnecessary stress for young students, research from the National Education Association shows that moderate homework assignments, when properly designed, can significantly improve retention and independent study skills."

**SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS FOR WEEKS 113-156**:
- Debate Cycle 1 (Weeks 113-120): Homework debate
- Debate Cycle 2 (Weeks 121-128): Video Games debate
- Debate Cycle 3 (Weeks 145-156): Smartphones debate
- Include fallacy detection examples (ad hominem, strawman, false dilemma)
- Synthesis from multiple sources (combine 2-3 perspectives)
- Independent research methodology
- Portfolio presentation skills

## XIII. AI TUTOR SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & REQUIREMENTS

### A. Rate Limiting & Fallback Chain

**Critical Configuration** (as of Jan 14, 2026):

#### 1. Groq API Rate Limiter

```javascript
class GroqRateLimiter {
  constructor() {
    this.requestsInWindow = 0;
    this.windowStartTime = Date.now();
    this.windowDuration = 60000;      // 1 minute rolling window
    this.maxRequests = 14;             // Conservative (Groq free tier: 15/min)
    this.backoffMs = 0;                // Exponential backoff tracking
  }
  
  async waitForSlot() {
    // Apply exponential backoff if set (after 429 errors)
    if (this.backoffMs > 0) {
      await sleep(this.backoffMs);
      this.backoffMs = Math.min(this.backoffMs * 2, 10000); // Max 10s
    }
    // ... quota checking logic
  }
  
  setBackoff(ms) {
    this.backoffMs = ms;
  }
  
  resetBackoff() {
    this.backoffMs = 0;
  }
}
```

**Key Parameters**:
- **maxRequests**: 14 per minute (safety buffer for 15/min limit)
- **Initial Backoff**: 2000ms (2 seconds) after first 429 error
- **Exponential Growth**: Doubles each 429 (2s → 4s → 8s → 10s max)
- **Window Reset**: Every 60 seconds

#### 2. Three-Layer Fallback Chain

```
Layer 1: Groq (llama-3.3-70b-versatile)
├─ Speed: Ultra-fast (<500ms)
├─ Quota: 14 requests/minute
└─ On Error (400/429/500) → Layer 2

Layer 2: Gemini 2.0 Flash
├─ Speed: Fast (~1-2s)
├─ Quota: Higher (Google Cloud)
└─ On Error → Layer 3

Layer 3: Deterministic Fallback
└─ Safe generic responses when both APIs fail
```

**Error Handling**:
- **429 (Rate Limit)**: Set 2s backoff, switch to Gemini
- **400 (Bad Request)**: Immediate switch to Gemini
- **500 (Server Error)**: Immediate switch to Gemini
- **Network Timeout**: After 10s, switch to fallback

### C. ACK + RECAST Mandatory Template

**Critical Requirement**: ALL AI Tutor responses MUST follow this 4-step formula.

#### The Formula

```
1. ACK (Acknowledge): Echo specific content from student's answer
2. RECAST (if needed): Model correct grammar naturally
3. ENCOURAGE: Brief, warm praise
4. ASK: One new open-ended question
```

#### Correct Examples

**Example 1**:
```
Student: "I have mother"

✅ CORRECT Response:
"You have a mother! That's wonderful! What does your mother do?"

Breakdown:
- ACK: "You have a mother" (echoes content + fixes grammar)
- RECAST: "a mother" (models article)
- ENCOURAGE: "That's wonderful!"
- ASK: "What does your mother do?"
```

**Example 2**:
```
Student: "My family big"

✅ CORRECT Response:
"Your family is big! That's great! How many people are in your family?"

Breakdown:
- ACK: "Your family is big" (echoes + adds "is")
- RECAST: Implicit (models correct "be" verb)
- ENCOURAGE: "That's great!"
- ASK: "How many people are in your family?"
```

**Example 3**:
```
Student: "I play with sister"

✅ CORRECT Response:
"You play with your sister! That sounds fun! What games do you play together?"

Breakdown:
- ACK: "You play with your sister" (echoes + adds "your")
- RECAST: Implicit (models possessive)
- ENCOURAGE: "That sounds fun!"
- ASK: "What games do you play together?"
```

#### Forbidden Patterns

```
❌ WRONG: "I heard you! What does your mother do?"
   Problem: Generic "I heard you" - no specific acknowledgment

❌ WRONG: "Great! I heard you! How many people?"
   Problem: No content echo, incomplete question

❌ WRONG: "That's good!"
   Problem: No acknowledgment of what student said

❌ WRONG: "Very nice! Now tell me about..."
   Problem: No specific content reference
```

#### Forbidden Phrases List

These phrases are **NEVER ALLOWED** in AI Tutor responses:

- "I heard you" (too generic)
- "I understand" (no content acknowledgment)
- "Great job!" (without echoing what was said)
- "Very good!" (without specific reference)
- "Excellent!" (standalone)
- "That's nice!" (vague)

#### Enforcement Mechanism

Location: `/src/services/ai_tutor/responseGuard.js`

```javascript
export function validateAckRecast(response) {
  const forbidden = [
    /\bI heard you\b/i,
    /\bGreat! I heard\b/i,
    /\bThat's good!\b(?!\s+\w)/  // Standalone only
  ];
  
  for (const pattern of forbidden) {
    if (pattern.test(response)) {
      return { valid: false, reason: 'Generic acknowledgment detected' };
    }
  }
  
  // Check for specific content echo
  // (implementation varies based on context)
  
  return { valid: true };
}
```

```javascript
      "Brothers and sisters play together",
      "Mothers and fathers take care of children",
      "Families eat together and talk",
      "Every family is different and special"
    ],
    
    example_opening_questions: [
      "Do you have a family?",
      "Who lives in your home?",
      "Do you have a mother?",
      "Do you have a father?",
      "Do you have brothers or sisters?",
      "Is your family big?",
      "Is your family happy?"
    ]
  }

```

#### 2. Opening Question Selection

**Algorithm**:
1. AI loads `freetalk_knowledge.example_opening_questions`
2. Randomly selects ONE question to start conversation
3. Must match week theme (not previous week's topic)
4. Must be A0 level (simple present, max 8 words)

**Why This Matters**:
- Ensures conversation starts on-theme
- Prevents Week 2 from asking Week 1 questions
- Gives AI clear direction for topic
- Maintains difficulty level consistency

#### 3. Conversation Flow

```
Turn 1: AI asks opening question from example_opening_questions
Turn 2: Student answers
Turn 3: AI applies ACK + RECAST, asks follow-up related to theme
Turn 4: Student answers
Turn 5: AI continues on theme using knowledge_base
... continues until student clicks END
```

**Knowledge Base Usage**:
- AI can reference ANY item from `knowledge_base` array
- Must stay within theme boundaries
- Can expand on topics naturally
- Should cycle through different aspects of theme

### E. Response Format Standards

#### 1. JSON Structure

ALL AI Tutor responses MUST return this exact structure:

```javascript
{
  teacher_ack: "string",              // ACK part only
  teacher_recast: "string",           // RECAST part (or same as ACK if perfect)
  teacher_encouragement: "string",    // ENCOURAGE part
  teacher_question: "string",         // ASK part
  
  // Hints for next expected answer
  hints: ["word1", "word2", "word3", "word4", "word5"],
  
  // Mission tracking (Story Mission only)
  mission_status: "in_progress" | "completed",
  current_turn: NUMBER,
  total_turns: NUMBER
}
```

#### 2. Hint Normalization Rules

**Before Sending to Frontend**:
```javascript
function normalizeHints(hints) {
  return hints
    .map(h => h.toLowerCase().trim())
    .filter(h => h.length > 0)
    .filter(h => !['um', 'uh', 'well', 'like'].includes(h))
    .slice(0, 6);  // Max 6 hints
}
```

**Forbidden Hints**:
- Filler words: "um", "uh", "well", "like"
- Punctuation-only: ".", ",", "!"
- Non-English: Vietnamese words (unless explicitly teaching them)
- Too advanced: Words above CEFR A0 level

#### 3. Mission Status Values

| Value | Meaning | When to Use |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| `in_progress` | Mission ongoing | current_turn < total_turns |
| `completed` | Mission finished | current_turn === total_turns |
| `error` | System error | API failure, invalid response |

### G. Common Pitfalls & Solutions

#### Pitfall 1: Week Detection Fails
**Symptom**: AI uses wrong week's theme in FreeTalk
**Cause**: Using `useParams()` outside Route tree
**Solution**: Use `useLocation()` + pathname parsing (see Section XIII.A.3)

#### Pitfall 2: Generic Acknowledgments
**Symptom**: "I heard you!" appears in responses
**Cause**: AI not following ACK + RECAST template
**Solution**: Enforce via responseGuard.js, update promptLibrary.js

#### Pitfall 3: Too Few Mission Turns
**Symptom**: Mission ends after 8-10 questions
**Cause**: Not following 15-turn minimum
**Solution**: Expand turnManager.js sequences to exactly 15 steps

#### Pitfall 4: Groq 429 Errors
**Symptom**: Frequent rate limiting despite limiter
**Cause**: maxRequests set too high
**Solution**: Reduce to 14 req/min (see Section XIII.A.1)

#### Pitfall 5: Yes/No Questions
**Symptom**: Students give one-word answers
**Cause**: Closed questions in turnManager.js
**Solution**: Rewrite all questions as open-ended Wh-questions
